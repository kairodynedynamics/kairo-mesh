Messages that still arrive when the network is gone.

Kairo Mesh is an embeddable engine that lets phones carry each other's encrypted messages with no internet, built for disaster, event and field apps.

Try the measurement yourself

Eighty people move between six neighbourhoods for eight hours. Three hundred messages are sent to people who are out of radio range, so they must be carried by passers-by. The simulation below runs in your browser on the same code as the repository.

"Spray-and-wait" with 4 copies is the courier rule published in the Bitchat whitepaper, reimplemented by us; it is not Bitchat's code. Kairo hands copies to carriers that meet the recipient more often. A single seed is noisy: the repository reports the mean of twenty.

What it does

Carriers chosen by encounter history. Matches the delivery rate of blind spraying while storing half as many copies, at the cost of somewhat longer delay.

Fountain-coded fragments. A photo is rebuilt from any sufficient set of packets from any relays; over a lossy link without feedback this needs 2 to 4.6 times fewer packets than plain fragments.

Rotating on-air identifiers and delivery receipts. Devices change their radio identity every fifteen minutes, and only the true recipient can make carriers drop their copies.

Where it stands

A research prototype: the algorithms, reproducible measurements, a node that chats over a local network, an Android app over Bluetooth LE (courier delivery verified on three emulators) and an iOS app that passes its tests on simulators. There is no field test on real phones and no independent security audit yet, so do not rely on it for safety.
